In the summer of 1969, Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin walked on the Moon while Michael Collins circled above in the command module Columbia. Back in Houston, Gene Kranz and Charlie Duke kept watch. The landing site, the Sea of Tranquility, was chosen after studying photos from Lunar Orbiter missions.

Years later, in Reykjavik, Iceland, a young engineer named Sigrun Thorsdottir visited the Kennedy Space Center in Florida. She met the daughter of Katherine Johnson at a conference in Hampton, Virginia, and heard how the human computers of NASA worked before electronic machines. Her favorite stop was Nairobi, where she talked with the astronomer Mukhtar Ouedraogo about the stars over Lake Victoria.

The path of an idea is never a straight line. It passes through Paris, Tokyo, Johannesburg, and Buenos Aires before it lands somewhere unexpected.
