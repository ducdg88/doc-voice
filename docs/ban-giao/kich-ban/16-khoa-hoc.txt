Sleep is not simply rest. While the body lies still, the brain is busy sorting the day. During deep sleep, waves of slow electrical activity wash through the cortex, and memories from the hippocampus are moved into long-term storage. During REM sleep, the brain replays emotional experiences and reconnects them with older knowledge.

Researchers at several universities have shown that people who sleep fewer than six hours a night perform about 30 percent worse on memory tests. Over time, chronic sleep loss raises the risk of heart disease, diabetes, and depression. A single night of poor sleep can increase hunger hormones and make high-calorie food look more attractive.

So what can you do? Keep a regular schedule, even on weekends. Avoid bright screens for an hour before bed. Keep the bedroom cool, dark, and quiet. Limit caffeine after noon, and avoid large meals late in the evening.

Most adults need between seven and nine hours. Children and teenagers need even more. If you often feel tired no matter how long you sleep, talk with a doctor, because conditions such as sleep apnea are common and treatable.

In short, sleep is the foundation. Everything else in your health is built on top of it.
